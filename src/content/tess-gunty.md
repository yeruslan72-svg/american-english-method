# Meet Tess Gunty: Listening to Many Voices in Contemporary America

**21st-Century American Writers · Tess Gunty · born 1993**

Tess Gunty is an American novelist from South Bend, Indiana. Her debut novel, *The Rabbit Hutch* (2022), won the National Book Award for Fiction. Through a group of neighbours living in a former industrial town, Gunty explores loneliness, community, social inequality, art, and the search for meaning.

---

## 1. Who Is Tess Gunty?

Tess Gunty was born and raised in South Bend, Indiana. She studied English and creative writing at the University of Notre Dame and later earned an MFA in Creative Writing from New York University, where she was a Lillian Vernon Fellow.

Her fiction and other writing have appeared in literary publications including *The Iowa Review*, *Joyland*, and the *Los Angeles Review of Books*. Her debut novel, *The Rabbit Hutch*, was published in 2022 and received the National Book Award for Fiction that year.

Gunty's work is especially interested in the lives people lead behind closed doors: their private hopes, disappointments, fears, and unexpected connections. She often combines close observation of ordinary life with unusual characters, shifting perspectives, and philosophical questions.

## 2. The Rabbit Hutch: A Portrait of a Midwestern Town

*The Rabbit Hutch* takes place in the fictional town of Vacca Vale, Indiana, a post-industrial community shaped by the decline of its automobile industry. Much of the story centres on an affordable housing building known as the Rabbit Hutch.

The novel follows several residents whose lives overlap. Among them are four young people who have recently left the foster-care system, a woman who writes online obituaries, a young mother, and a former child star. Their stories are different, but they share the same building and the same uncertain world.

The novel moves between characters rather than following only one central voice. This structure allows the reader to see how different people experience the same place.

### What Does “Post-Industrial” Mean?

A post-industrial town is a place whose economy has changed after major industries — such as car manufacturing, steel production, or mining — have declined or moved away. Such places may face job losses, empty factories, population decline, and uncertainty about the future.

Vacca Vale is fictional, but its setting reflects real questions about communities across the American Midwest.

## 3. Many Voices, One Community

One of the novel's most distinctive features is its many points of view. Instead of asking readers to follow only one person's journey, Gunty moves among different characters and gradually reveals their connections.

This is sometimes called **polyphonic fiction**. The word comes from music: a polyphonic composition contains several independent melodic lines. In a novel, it can describe a story built from multiple voices and perspectives.

The technique asks readers to listen carefully. A person who seems strange or distant at first may become understandable when the reader learns more about that person's history.

### A Useful Reading Question

When a novel changes its point of view, ask:

- Whose thoughts can we hear now?
- What does this character know that the others do not?
- Does the new perspective change our judgement of an earlier event?
- What connects this character to the wider community?

## 4. Themes: Loneliness, Freedom, and Attention

Several themes run through *The Rabbit Hutch*:

- **Loneliness and connection** — people can live close to one another and still feel profoundly alone.
- **Community** — neighbours may be connected by place even when they barely know each other.
- **Social inequality** — the novel asks how opportunity, safety, and security are distributed.
- **Freedom and confinement** — characters search for ways to escape the roles or circumstances that limit them.
- **Attention and empathy** — noticing another person's experience can challenge our assumptions.

Gunty does not present the town as a simple symbol of decline. Its residents are complicated individuals, capable of humour, tenderness, selfishness, imagination, and change.

## 5. Tess Gunty's Literary Approach

Gunty combines elements of literary realism with comic, experimental, and philosophical writing. Her characters may behave in surprising ways, but the novel asks readers to take their inner lives seriously.

A particularly important feature is the relationship between the individual and the place. The building, the town, its abandoned industrial spaces, and its residents all contribute to the story. The setting is not merely a background; it helps shape the characters' possibilities.

The novel's structure is also important. By shifting among several characters, Gunty creates a wider picture of a community than a single narrator might provide.

## 6. Language in Context

| Word or expression | Meaning |
|---|---|
| post-industrial | relating to a place or economy after the decline of major industries |
| affordable housing | housing offered at a cost considered manageable for people with limited or moderate incomes |
| resident | a person who lives in a particular place |
| point of view | the position or perspective from which a story is told |
| perspective | a particular way of seeing or understanding something |
| polyphonic | containing several independent voices or perspectives |
| to overlap | to share a part or happen at the same time |
| to feel isolated | to feel separated from other people |
| sense of belonging | the feeling of being accepted as part of a group or place |
| to challenge an assumption | to question something accepted as true |
| to pay attention to | to notice or give careful thought to something |
| to make ends meet | to have just enough money to pay for basic needs |

**Natural American English examples:**

- The story follows several residents of the same apartment building.
- Their lives overlap in unexpected ways.
- The town has struggled since its factories closed.
- She sometimes feels isolated, even when other people are nearby.
- The novel challenges our assumptions about people we do not know.
- Many families are working hard to make ends meet.

## 9. Put It Into Practice

### Speaking Task

Imagine you are describing your neighbourhood to someone who has never visited it. Speak for one minute. Mention:

- what kinds of buildings you see;
- where people meet;
- what has changed in recent years;
- what makes the area feel like a community.

Try to use the expressions **sense of belonging**, **residents**, and **to make ends meet**.

### Writing Task

Write a short paragraph answering this question:

**Can people feel lonely even when they live in a busy community?**

Give one reason and one example. Aim for 100–130 words.

## 10. Connect the Writers

Tess Gunty's novel is rooted in the contemporary American Midwest. This makes it a useful companion to other writers in our American Writers collection:

- **John Steinbeck** often wrote about working people, economic hardship, and communities under pressure.
- **Ernest Hemingway** is known for concise prose and characters facing difficult choices.
- **Percival Everett** uses satire, changing perspectives, and literary reimagining to question familiar stories.

These writers belong to different generations and have very different styles. Comparing them helps us see how American literature continues to change.

## 11. Takeaway

*The Rabbit Hutch* asks readers to look beyond first impressions. A building is more than a collection of rooms, and a community is more than a group of strangers living near one another. By giving several characters their own voices, Tess Gunty invites us to notice the hidden lives that make up contemporary America.
