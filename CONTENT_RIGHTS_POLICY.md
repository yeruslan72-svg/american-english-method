# AEM Content Rights & Attribution Policy

**Version:** 1.0  
**Adopted:** 20 November 2026  
**Applies to:** American English Method (AEM), including the public website, lessons, audio, images, video, translations, downloadable resources, social channels, and future paid or subscription-based services.

## 1. Purpose and governing principle

AEM uses reliable sources to teach American English and to introduce learners to American life, history, culture, arts, science, technology, and society. We respect creators, rights holders, learners, and the law.

**A source being public, educational, accessible without payment, or easy to download does not mean its content is free to copy or adapt. Attribution is not a substitute for permission.** Every third-party asset must have a documented legal basis for the exact way AEM intends to use it.

This policy is an internal editorial control, not a legal opinion or a guarantee that every use is lawful in every country. Where applicable law, license wording, ownership, or permitted use is uncertain, the material stays on hold until the issue is resolved.

## 2. Scope: what must be checked

The policy applies to each distinct third-party item, including photographs, illustrations, maps, diagrams, charts, infographics, audio recordings, songs, sound effects, podcasts, transcripts, video, film excerpts, animations, screenshots, poems, literary extracts, stories, articles, translations, subtitles, adaptations, datasets, teaching materials, logos, trademarks, recognizable people, and other elements that may carry separate rights.

A webpage may contain several works with different owners and licenses. Rights must be checked at item level, not inferred from the page as a whole.

## 3. Acceptable rights bases

A material may be approved only when at least one of the following is documented and covers the intended use:

1. **Original AEM material.** Created by the AEM team without incorporating protected third-party expression, or created under written assignment/permission covering AEM's intended use.
2. **Public domain.** The exact work is verified as public domain in the relevant jurisdiction(s), including applicable term, publication, authorship, and government-work rules. Record the evidence and jurisdiction.
3. **Open license.** The exact license and version are identified, and all conditions are met. For a commercial AEM service, a NonCommercial (NC) restriction is not acceptable. A NoDerivatives (ND) restriction does not permit a translation or adaptation where that would constitute a derivative. ShareAlike (SA) material requires a compatibility review before adaptation or combination with AEM content.
4. **Written permission or commercial license.** The rights holder has granted permission covering AEM's actual use, including commercial/paid access where relevant, territories, duration, format, hosting/streaming, and adaptation/translation if needed.
5. **A specific legal exception.** A documented, case-specific review concludes that the planned use is permitted under applicable law. Do not assume that an educational purpose, short excerpt, or credit line automatically qualifies as fair use or another exception.

If none applies, do not publish, reproduce, translate, upload, or embed the material.

## 4. Commercial-use default

AEM may become a paid educational platform. Therefore, the default screening standard is permission for commercial use, including subscription access, paid courses, and other monetized distribution.

Unless a documented exception is approved, exclude CC licenses containing NC; licenses or terms that prohibit the intended redistribution, public display, streaming, or hosting; ND material if AEM intends to translate, subtitle, edit, abridge, or adapt it; material whose license is unclear, expired, withdrawn, or not traceable to the exact item; and content copied from search results, social media, blogs, news pages, or video platforms without a verified rights basis.

A free-to-view or free-to-download item is not automatically commercially reusable.

## 5. Embedding, linking, and copying are different

- **Linking:** Prefer linking learners to the original publisher when sufficient. Do not bypass paywalls, access controls, or technical restrictions.
- **Embedding/streaming:** Use an official player or embed only when the publisher's terms and the specific asset's rights permit the planned use. An available embed button is not, by itself, proof that all commercial uses are permitted.
- **Local hosting/republication:** Downloading an asset into the AEM repository or serving it from AEM is a separate use and requires rights for reproduction, hosting, and distribution.
- **Transcripts and excerpts:** A transcript, subtitle, translation, or excerpt can involve separate reproduction and adaptation rights. A link to the original transcript does not authorize copying it into AEM.

## 6. Attribution standard

Where attribution is required, or is appropriate as good practice, record and display as applicable: title; creator/author/photographer/performer/rights holder; original source URL; exact license name and version or permission reference; link to license/rights statement; concise note describing changes (crop, edit, translation, excerpt, subtitles, adaptation); and required copyright notice or wording.

Use the license's exact attribution requirements. AEM's preferred internal checklist is **TASL**: Title, Author, Source, License. Attribution must not imply sponsorship, endorsement, or affiliation unless expressly authorized.

## 7. Separate rights and special cases

A copyright license may not clear all rights in an asset. Check separately where relevant: privacy, publicity, model, and property releases for recognizable people or private locations; trademarks, logos, product designs, and brand endorsement implications; artwork, posters, book covers, sculptures, and other works visible inside photographs; music, lyrics, performances, and sound recordings included in video or audio; and third-party images, quotations, charts, or clips incorporated into an otherwise reusable article or government publication.

Do not assume that all material on a government, museum, university, archive, or public broadcaster website is public domain or commercially reusable. Check the individual item and its rights statement.

## 8. Original writing and factual research

AEM should prefer independently written explanations, lesson dialogues, examples, questions, and summaries based on verified facts.

- Facts, ideas, and historical events may be researched from reliable sources; write the explanation independently rather than copying a source's wording or structure.
- Keep citations and research notes for factual claims, especially dates, statistics, scientific claims, quotations, and contested historical interpretations.
- Do not translate a protected article, story, poem, or dialogue and publish the translation without permission or a clearly applicable legal basis.
- For literary and artistic works, verify the status of the exact text, edition, translation, illustration, and recording separately. A public-domain original does not automatically make a modern translation or recording public domain.
- Use quotations only when documented permission or an applicable legal exception supports the particular use. There is no universal word-count safe harbor.

## 9. Required review workflow

Before an asset is published:

1. Identify the exact item and its original source.
2. Identify the author/creator and current rights holder where possible.
3. Open and read the item's own license, rights statement, or written permission.
4. Check the exact version and restrictions, especially NC, ND, SA, territory, attribution, and redistribution.
5. Match the rights granted to the intended AEM use: commercial access, display, streaming, local hosting, reproduction, excerpting, translation, adaptation, and modification.
6. Check separate privacy, publicity, trademark, and third-party-content issues.
7. Record the evidence, date checked, intended lesson/page, attribution wording, and reviewer in CONTENT_RIGHTS_REGISTER.md.
8. Add or update the appropriate public-facing credit in IMAGE_CREDITS.md, AUDIO_SOURCES.md, or the relevant lesson.
9. Assign a status:
   - **APPROVED** — evidence supports the exact planned use.
   - **CONDITIONAL** — permitted only under stated conditions; all conditions must be implemented.
   - **HOLD** — evidence is incomplete or rights are uncertain; do not publish/use until resolved.
   - **REMOVED** — no longer used; retain the record for audit history.

If an asset changes, the source terms change, or the use changes (for example, from external streaming to local hosting), review it again.

## 10. Existing material and audit rule

Previously published or recorded material is not automatically grandfathered in. Existing entries in IMAGE_CREDITS.md, AUDIO_SOURCES.md, lesson pages, and other registers must be reconciled with this policy.

A source register entry is evidence of tracking, not by itself proof of permission. If the precise asset URL, license version, rights statement, or permitted use is missing, mark the entry HOLD or CONDITIONAL — recheck required until verified. Do not silently treat older credits as final legal clearance.

## 11. Records and responsibility

- CONTENT_RIGHTS_REGISTER.md is the central decision register.
- IMAGE_CREDITS.md remains the detailed image-credit inventory.
- AUDIO_SOURCES.md remains the detailed audio-source inventory.
- Lesson pages must display credits required by the relevant license or permission.
- The person adding or commissioning an asset is responsible for supplying its source and rights evidence before publication.
- Keep copies or stable links to license terms and written permissions where possible. Record the date checked because terms and webpages can change.
