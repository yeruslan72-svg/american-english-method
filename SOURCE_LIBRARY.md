## 1. Purpose

The AEM Source Library is the working catalogue of reliable sources for developing lessons and cultural material about the United States.

It supports the following AEM directions:

- American History & Heritage.
- American Culture & Arts.
- Modern America & Innovation.
- Everyday Life in America.
- American Society & Mindset.
- American Science & Discoveries.
- Great Americans & Inventors.
- American Companies & Industry.
- American Places & Landmarks.
- Sports & Entertainment.

The catalogue is designed for research, fact-checking, lesson planning, and the discovery of media that may be lawfully reused in a commercial educational product.

**The central rule: reliability and reuse rights are separate questions.** A trustworthy institution may host an image, recording, article, or exhibit created by a third party. The institution's reputation does not automatically grant AEM permission to copy that item.

## 2. How to read source status

| Status | Meaning | AEM use |
|---|---|---|
| RESEARCH-READY | Institution is a suitable primary or authoritative source for the subject described | Use to verify facts; write explanations independently and cite the source |
| REUSE-READY — ITEM CHECK REQUIRED | The institution has a clearly defined reuse programme or public-domain policy for a subset of material | Use only assets whose individual item page and rights statement match the intended commercial use |
| PERMISSION REQUIRED / HOLD | The source includes protected, licensed, or third-party content, or its reuse rules do not clearly permit commercial use | Link to the source or request written permission; do not copy into AEM until cleared |
| RESEARCH LEAD | Potentially useful institution or dataset not yet reviewed in sufficient detail | Research only after checking the specific source and methodology |

No institution-level status is a blanket clearance for all content on that institution's website.

## 3. Core source catalogue

### 3.1. American history, primary documents, photographs and newspapers

#### Library of Congress (LOC)
- Main collections: https://www.loc.gov/
- Free to Use and Reuse: https://www.loc.gov/free-to-use/
- Copyright guidance: https://www.loc.gov/legal/security-copyright-and-privacy/understanding-copyright/
- Chronicling America: https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/

**Best for:** American history, founding documents, presidential papers, historical photographs, maps, posters, newspapers, music history, literature, immigration, regional life, architecture, and everyday life across different periods.

**Research value:** A major national library and primary-source collection. Use catalogue records, collection essays, dates, creator information, and item metadata to verify claims.

**Reuse position:**
- The curated *Free to Use and Reuse* sets are the preferred starting point for images and other media intended for reuse.
- For any other item, read its individual Rights and Access statement. The Library of Congress often does not own copyright in collection materials and cannot grant permission on behalf of rights holders.
- Its guidance distinguishes public-domain material, CC0, permission-based material, and items with unknown or other restrictions.
- Chronicling America describes U.S. newspapers published more than 95 years ago as public domain in their entirety; more recent newspapers can contain third-party material. Verify the particular issue and date.

**AEM rule:** Prioritize individual items explicitly marked *Free to Use and Reuse*, *Public Domain*, or with a suitable open license. Keep the item URL, creator, title, date, rights statement, and credit line in the content record. For uncertain items, link rather than reproduce.

**Status:** RESEARCH-READY; REUSE-READY — ITEM CHECK REQUIRED.

#### National Archives and Records Administration (NARA)
- National Archives Catalog: https://catalog.archives.gov/
- Copyright and Permissions: https://www.archives.gov/research/still-pictures/permissions
- Frequently Asked Questions: https://www.archives.gov/faqs

**Best for:** Founding and constitutional history, presidents, Congress, federal agencies, wars, civil rights, immigration, public policy, government records, historical photographs, posters, films, and sound recordings.

**Research value:** Primary federal records and archival descriptions. Prefer the original record and its catalogue metadata over secondary summaries.

**Reuse position:**
- NARA states that the vast majority of digital images in its Catalog are public domain and may be used without written permission; credit to NARA is appreciated.
- Check the Catalog record's *Use Restriction(s)* field.
- Not all NARA holdings are public domain. Special Media and donated or third-party materials can carry copyright, donor, contract, publicity, or other restrictions.
- NARA does not certify an item's copyright status or grant a general license to all records.

**AEM rule:** Record the exact Catalog item and its *Use Restriction(s)* field. Do not assume that a record is cleared solely because NARA holds it.

**Status:** RESEARCH-READY; REUSE-READY — ITEM CHECK REQUIRED.

#### National Endowment for the Humanities (NEH)
- Main site: https://www.neh.gov/
- Privacy Policy and Terms of Use: https://www.neh.gov/privacy-terms-of-use
- Public Access: https://www.neh.gov/publicaccess

**Best for:** Humanities research, American history, literature, philosophy, cultural heritage, museums, public humanities projects, and research grants.

**Research value:** Useful for discovering funded research, scholarly projects, public humanities programmes, and subject experts. Follow the cited research or publication to its original evidence.

**Reuse position:** NEH states that federal-agency material is generally public domain, but artwork and other materials on its website may be protected and provided only for personal, educational, informational, and non-commercial use. Unauthorized commercial use of protected material is prohibited unless the rights holder grants prior written permission.

**AEM rule:** Use NEH as a research and discovery source. Do not copy its protected images, artwork, or contributed materials into a paid course without explicit commercial permission.

**Status:** RESEARCH-READY; PERMISSION REQUIRED / HOLD for protected media.

### 3.2. Museums, art, literature and cultural heritage

#### Smithsonian Institution — Open Access
- Open Access portal: https://www.si.edu/openaccess
- Open Access FAQ: https://www.si.edu/openaccess/faq
- Terms of Use: https://www.si.edu/termsofuse

**Best for:** American art, design, history, cultural heritage, science, technology, natural history, material culture, photography, objects, and biographies.

**Research value:** A network of museums, research centres, libraries, archives, and the National Zoo. Use collection records and curatorial descriptions to establish object identity, creator, date, context, and provenance.

**Reuse position:**
- Smithsonian digital assets specifically marked with the CC0 symbol may be used commercially without Smithsonian permission, fees, or required attribution.
- The CC0 designation applies only to the marked asset. Third-party rights, privacy, publicity, trademarks, and other legal restrictions can still apply.
- Other Smithsonian website content is subject to separate usage conditions. The general Terms restrict commercial use of content not marked CC0 unless permission is obtained.
- Do not treat Smithsonian names, logos, or trademarks as CC0 assets.

**AEM rule:** Search the Open Access portal and retain the exact object page and CC0 designation. Do not download an image from an ordinary museum page and assume it is covered by Open Access.

**Status:** RESEARCH-READY; REUSE-READY — CC0 ITEMS ONLY, WITH THIRD-PARTY RIGHTS CHECK.

#### National Park Service (NPS)
- Main site: https://www.nps.gov/
- Publications and copyright notice: https://www.nps.gov/aboutus/publications.htm
- National Register content and copyright: https://www.nps.gov/subjects/nationalregister/national-register-content-and-copyright.htm

**Best for:** National parks, American landscapes, geography, conservation, Indigenous and local histories, historic buildings, monuments, battlefields, and public heritage.

**Research value:** Official park histories, interpretive materials, historic-place descriptions, conservation information, and geographic context.

**Reuse position:**
- Government information on NPS websites is generally public domain and may be copied with acknowledgement.
- NPS websites also contain photographs, documents, and other materials contributed or licensed by third parties.
- NPS asks users to credit public-domain images to the National Park Service; third-party images require permission from their rights holders.

**AEM rule:** Use NPS facts and official public-domain material with source credit. Check each photograph, map, document, and historic-register record for third-party credits and restrictions.

**Status:** RESEARCH-READY; REUSE-READY — ITEM CHECK REQUIRED.

### 3.3. Science, technology, space, environment and innovation

#### NASA
- NASA Images and Media Usage Guidelines: https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/
- NASA Image and Video Library: https://images.nasa.gov/

**Best for:** Space exploration, astronomy, Earth observation, engineering, aeronautics, scientific missions, technology, discoveries, and the history of NASA programmes.

**Research value:** Mission pages, scientific explanations, image metadata, mission timelines, and publications. For scientific claims, follow links to the underlying mission data, papers, or technical documentation.

**Reuse position:**
- NASA states that its own images, audio, and video are generally not subject to copyright in the United States and may be used factually, subject to its guidelines.
- NASA occasionally uses third-party copyrighted material. Such items are identified with the copyright holder; NASA's permission does not pass to AEM.
- NASA insignia, logotype, identifiers, and employee likenesses are subject to separate restrictions. Commercial use must not imply NASA endorsement. Promotional or cover-art uses involving NASA logos or employees may require clearances.

**AEM rule:** Check every asset's credit line and copyright marking. Do not use NASA logos as AEM decoration or imply NASA endorsement. Prefer factual editorial use and identify NASA as the source.

**Status:** RESEARCH-READY; REUSE-READY — NASA-OWNED ITEMS ONLY, WITH ITEM AND ENDORSEMENT CHECK.

#### National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA)
- Main site: https://www.noaa.gov/
- National Ocean Service image-use FAQ: https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/about/faq.html
- NOAA image licensing and usage information: https://www.omao.noaa.gov/image-licensing-usage-info
- NOAA Digital Photo Collection: https://photolib.noaa.gov/

**Best for:** Oceans, weather, climate, hurricanes, coastlines, marine life, fisheries, navigation, satellites, environmental science, and ocean exploration.

**Research value:** Government science, measurements, reports, datasets, forecasts, historical records, and specialist educational explanations. Distinguish observed data, model outputs, forecasts, and interpretations.

**Reuse position:**
- NOAA says most of its information is public domain, but item captions and credits must be checked.
- NOAA videos frequently contain third-party footage and require case-by-case review.
- Identifiable people may have privacy or publicity rights. NOAA emblems and marks are not automatically available for unrestricted use.
- Do not modify content in a way that misrepresents it as official NOAA material or imply government endorsement.

**AEM rule:** Prefer NOAA-owned still photographs, diagrams, and data with clear credits. Review video, music, narration, recognizable people, and third-party credits separately.

**Status:** RESEARCH-READY; REUSE-READY — ITEM CHECK REQUIRED.

#### U.S. Geological Survey (USGS)
- Main site: https://www.usgs.gov/
- Copyrights and Credits: https://www.usgs.gov/information-policies-and-instructions/copyrights-and-credits
- Publications Warehouse: https://pubs.usgs.gov/

**Best for:** Geology, earthquakes, volcanoes, water, ecosystems, natural hazards, maps, landforms, and scientific data.

**Research value:** Technical reports, maps, datasets, scientific explanations, and measurements. Check the publication date, geographic scope, methodology, and whether data are preliminary or final.

**Reuse position:**
- USGS-authored or produced information is generally in the U.S. public domain; acknowledgement is requested.
- Some photographs, graphics, and illustrations on USGS pages are owned by third parties and are generally marked as copyrighted.
- USGS personnel likenesses or voices may raise publicity-rights issues in commercial uses.

**AEM rule:** Use USGS facts, data, and USGS-created media with clear credit. Do not reuse a third-party image merely because it appears in a USGS report or page.

**Status:** RESEARCH-READY; REUSE-READY — USGS-OWNED ITEMS ONLY, WITH ITEM CHECK.

#### National Science Foundation (NSF)
- Main site: https://www.nsf.gov/
- Web Policies: https://www.nsf.gov/policies/digital

**Best for:** Scientific research, discoveries, research funding, STEM education, engineering, computing, climate science, and profiles of researchers and institutions.

**Research value:** Research announcements, award records, project descriptions, science explainers, and links to funded research. Verify major scientific claims against the original paper, dataset, or research institution.

**Reuse position:**
- NSF says most text on its pages is federal-government work or material prepared under terms that place it in the public domain; identified copyrighted text is an exception.
- NSF visual media — photos, illustrations, and graphics — may be public domain, contractor-created, or licensed from others. NSF explicitly says visual-media reuse is granted case by case and should not be assumed without permission.
- NSF logos and identifiable people require separate care.

**AEM rule:** Use NSF primarily for research discovery and factual background. Link to visual media unless NSF confirms in writing that the specific item is available for AEM's commercial use.

**Status:** RESEARCH-READY; PERMISSION REQUIRED / HOLD for visual media unless individually cleared.

### 3.4. Society, population, work and the economy — research leads

These sources are useful for factual context in *Modern America & Innovation*. They are listed as research leads, not as blanket content licenses.

#### U.S. Census Bureau
- https://www.census.gov/
- Data portal: https://data.census.gov/

**Best for:** Population, households, migration, housing, education, communities, business counts, and demographic change.

**AEM method:** Cite the dataset, table, geography, year, and release. Explain what the statistic measures and what it does not measure. Avoid turning population-level data into claims about every individual American.

**Rights note:** Verify the provenance and rights statement of each downloadable dataset, map, chart, photograph, or third-party contribution. Do not assume every visual asset on a federal website is government-owned.

**Status:** RESEARCH LEAD — dataset and item-level review.

#### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)
- https://www.bls.gov/
- Data tools: https://www.bls.gov/data/

**Best for:** Employment, occupations, wages, inflation, working conditions, productivity, and labor-market trends.

**AEM method:** Always state the indicator, population, period, geography, and whether figures are nominal, adjusted, preliminary, or seasonally adjusted where relevant.

**Rights note:** Prefer original statistical tables and cite the dataset. Check third-party content, graphics, and image credits separately.

**Status:** RESEARCH LEAD — dataset and item-level review.

#### Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA)
- https://www.bea.gov/
- Data: https://www.bea.gov/data

**Best for:** GDP, personal income, consumer spending, regional economies, international trade, and industry-level economic activity.

**AEM method:** Explain the indicator in plain language and give the period and release date. Do not present one statistic as a complete measure of prosperity or quality of life.

**Rights note:** Cite the specific BEA table or release. Review individual media and third-party material separately.

**Status:** RESEARCH LEAD — dataset and item-level review.

## 4. Editorial source hierarchy

When preparing a factual lesson, use this order of preference:

1. **Primary evidence:** original records, official datasets, scientific papers, historical documents, direct statements, collection records, and original artefacts.
2. **Authoritative institutional explanation:** museums, archives, universities, government scientific agencies, and established research institutions.
3. **Specialist secondary analysis:** peer-reviewed scholarship, academic books, and subject-specific research.
4. **Responsible journalism:** use for contemporary context and leads; verify important factual claims against primary or independent sources.
5. **General web content and social media:** use only as leads, not as sole evidence for important factual claims.

A government source is authoritative for its own records, statistics, and programmes, but an official website is not automatically neutral about every policy or historical interpretation. Where subjects are contested, compare credible sources and distinguish evidence, interpretation, and opinion.

## 5. AEM research and writing rules

- Write original explanations. Do not copy the source's prose, distinctive structure, lesson text, captions, charts, or presentation.
- Separate documented facts from interpretation, opinion, and illustrative examples.
- For historical subjects, identify dates, places, participants, and the type of source used. Where possible, use at least one primary source and one reputable scholarly or institutional interpretation.
- For science, identify the research institution, publication or dataset, date, and whether findings are established, preliminary, disputed, or still under investigation.
- For social and economic topics, identify the population, geography, time period, measure, and limitations of the data.
- Avoid stereotypes and universal claims about Americans. Explain regional, generational, cultural, and socioeconomic diversity.
- Do not use a photograph, recording, chart, or quotation just because it appears on a reliable source website.
- Prefer linking to protected material over copying it.
- Attribution is not a substitute for permission.
- Do not assume that U.S. public-domain status automatically resolves every legal issue in every country where AEM may be accessed. Consider applicable local law and separate privacy, publicity, trademark, and contract restrictions.

## 6. Item-level clearance checklist

Before an external asset is published, record:

- [ ] Unique AEM asset ID.
- [ ] Exact title and description.
- [ ] Creator / photographer / author.
- [ ] Original source page and direct asset URL.
- [ ] Item-level rights statement or exact license.
- [ ] Commercial use permitted for the intended context.
- [ ] Copying, downloading, local hosting, streaming, and distribution rights.
- [ ] Editing, cropping, subtitles, translation, or adaptation rights, as applicable.
- [ ] Third-party content, people, voices, logos, trademarks, artwork, and property checked.
- [ ] Required attribution and source link.
- [ ] Date checked and evidence retained.
- [ ] Entry added to `CONTENT_RIGHTS_REGISTER.md` and relevant source inventory.

If any essential permission or restriction remains unclear, the asset status is **HOLD**. Do not publish it as cleared.

## 8. Review schedule

- Review source terms before first commercial reuse of an asset.
- Recheck terms when an asset, use, or source changes.
- Recheck research claims when a lesson is materially updated or when new evidence appears.
- For statistics, record the data release date and revisit figures that are likely to change.
- Keep old entries and decisions in the register rather than silently overwriting the audit trail.
