## 1. Purpose

American English Method is a practical language-learning framework designed to help learners understand and use contemporary American English in real-life communication.

The project focuses on:

- Natural everyday communication.
- Contextual vocabulary and expressions.
- Grammar in practical use.
- Listening comprehension and connected speech.
- American cultural and social context.
- Regional language variation, including selected features of Southern American English.
- Independent speaking and the ability to express personal thoughts.

The purpose of this document is to establish the methodological principles, reference sources, verification procedures, and editorial standards used throughout the project.

It provides a common foundation for lesson development and future revisions.

**Our guiding principle:**

> Language should be learned as a living means of communication, not merely as a collection of grammar rules and vocabulary lists.

---

## 2. Methodological Foundation

American English Method follows a communicative, context-based approach to language learning.

The learner is encouraged to understand how language works in actual situations and to develop the ability to communicate independently.

The method draws on principles associated with the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR), particularly its emphasis on communicative language activities, interaction, mediation, pragmatic competence, and sociolinguistic awareness.

The project does not claim to be an official CEFR course, an accredited examination programme, or a formally validated language assessment system.

CEFR is used as a reference framework, not as a certification claim.

### 2.1. Five Learning Directions

The course is organized around five complementary learning directions:

1. **Everyday Scenarios** — practical communication in common situations.
2. **City & Daily Life** — navigating everyday environments and social interactions.
3. **Descriptive Lessons** — describing objects, places, surroundings, and experiences.
4. **Southern English** — understanding selected regional expressions, pronunciation, grammar, and cultural context.
5. **Connected Speech** — recognizing linking, reductions, elision, rhythm, and natural spoken pronunciation.

These directions represent complementary layers of language learning. They may be studied in parallel rather than as strictly separated stages.

### 2.2. Context Before Memorization

Vocabulary is introduced through situations, dialogues, descriptions, and personal expression.

Learners are encouraged to understand:

- What an expression means.
- When it is appropriate.
- Who might use it.
- What tone it conveys.
- Whether it is neutral, informal, regional, dated, or stylistically marked.

Memorizing a translation without understanding its context is not considered sufficient learning.

### 2.3. Active Language Production

The course emphasizes independent language production.

Learners should progressively move from recognizing expressions to using them in their own sentences, conversations, and descriptions.

The preferred learning cycle is:

**Practice → Learner's Response → Correction → Natural Reformulation → Recall**

The purpose is not simply to identify mistakes, but to help learners develop natural, confident, and contextually appropriate communication.

## 3. Reference Sources

The project uses established linguistic, educational, and reference resources.

No single dictionary, corpus, textbook, or academic publication is treated as the sole authority on all aspects of American English.

Different sources are used for different purposes.

### 3.1. Language Education and Communicative Competence

**Council of Europe — Common European Framework of Reference for Languages: Learning, Teaching, Assessment. Companion Volume (2020).**

Official resource:

https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-companion-volume-and-its-language-versions

Purpose:

- Communicative language competence.
- Interaction and mediation.
- Pragmatic and sociolinguistic competence.
- Language learning objectives.
- Development of practical communication skills.

The CEFR provides a broad educational reference framework. It does not prescribe the specific lesson structure or vocabulary of American English Method.

### 3.2. Grammar and Language Usage

**Cambridge English Grammar Today**

https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/

Purpose:

- Grammatical structures.
- Spoken and written language.
- Grammar in context.
- Common usage patterns.
- Differences between formal and informal expression.

Cambridge English Grammar Today is a supplementary grammar reference. Its examples and explanations should not automatically be treated as exclusively American usage.

**Merriam-Webster Dictionary**

https://www.merriam-webster.com/

Purpose:

- American English vocabulary.
- Word meanings and distinctions.
- Pronunciation.
- Usage labels.
- Informal and regional expressions.
- Idiomatic language.

Particular attention should be paid to dictionary usage labels and distinctions between individual senses of a word.

### 3.3. Corpus-Based Language Verification

**Corpus of Contemporary American English (COCA)**

https://www.english-corpora.org/coca/

Purpose:

- Examining real examples of American English.
- Comparing expressions in different contexts.
- Investigating collocations.
- Exploring spoken and written usage.
- Observing differences across genres.

The COCA version documented by its provider contains approximately one billion words from 1990–2019 across eight genres, including spoken language, fiction, magazines, newspapers, academic texts, blogs, other web pages, and television and movie subtitles.

Corpus data is used to investigate language patterns, not to establish absolute rules.

A frequent expression is not necessarily appropriate in every situation, and an infrequent expression is not necessarily incorrect.

Corpus results must be interpreted in context.

### 3.4. Southern American English

**Kirk Hazen — English in the U.S. South. Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Linguistics (2022).**

https://doi.org/10.1093/acrefore/9780199384655.013.925

**Becky Childs and Paul E. Reed — English in the Southern United States. The New Cambridge History of the English Language (2026).**

https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009205771.012

Purpose:

- Understanding regional language variation.
- Identifying linguistic features associated with the American South.
- Distinguishing regional and social variation.
- Avoiding stereotypes about Southern speakers.
- Understanding that Southern English is not a single uniform variety.

Southern expressions and pronunciation features are included selectively, with attention to modern usage and context.

The project does not present a fictional or exaggerated Southern accent as a model of authentic American speech.

## 4. Language Verification Principles

Every lesson should distinguish between four separate questions.

### 4.1. Grammatical Correctness

Is the expression grammatically acceptable?

A sentence should not be classified as incorrect simply because another formulation is more conversational.

### 4.2. Conversational Naturalness

Would the expression be reasonably natural in the intended situation?

Naturalness depends on context, relationship, register, tone, and speaker intention.

An expression may be grammatically correct but unnecessarily formal, vague, or unusual in a particular conversation.

### 4.3. Regional and Social Variation

Is the expression nationwide, regional, socially marked, or associated with a particular speech community?

Regional language should be identified accurately and without exaggeration.

### 4.4. Cultural Interpretation

Does the explanation describe a documented cultural pattern, a common social convention, or an interpretation?

Cultural observations must not be presented as universal rules applying to every American.

## 5. Editorial Standards

All lessons should follow these principles.

### Principle 1 — Correctness Is Not the Same as Naturalness

A grammatically correct sentence must not be labelled wrong merely because a more natural conversational alternative exists.

The distinction between an error and a stylistic improvement must be explicit.

### Principle 2 — Context Determines Appropriate Usage

An expression should be evaluated in its intended situation.

The same words may be appropriate among friends but unsuitable in a formal meeting, professional email, or unfamiliar social setting.

### Principle 3 — Regional Does Not Mean Universal

An expression associated with Texas or the American South must not automatically be presented as typical of the entire United States.

### Principle 4 — Cultural Observations Require Qualification

Avoid statements such as:

- All Americans say...
- Americans always...
- People in the South never...
- This expression is used only in Texas...

Prefer qualified descriptions:

- Commonly used in informal American English.
- Often associated with Southern speech.
- May sound regional or old-fashioned.
- Usage varies by region and speaker.
- More typical in certain social contexts.

### Principle 5 — Examples Are Not Statistical Evidence

An example demonstrates a possible use of language. It does not establish frequency or universal acceptance.

Frequency claims require appropriate supporting evidence.

### Principle 6 — Authenticity Over Stereotypes

The project aims to teach contemporary American English.

Southern expressions, contractions, colloquial language, and connected speech should be included where they add genuine communicative value.

They should not be inserted merely to create an artificial accent.

### Principle 7 — Author-Created Material Must Be Identifiable

Dialogues, scenarios, exercises, and explanatory examples created for the course are original educational material.

They must not be represented as:

- Verbatim recordings of real conversations.
- Direct quotations from native speakers.
- Corpus examples unless their source is documented.
- Published linguistic research unless correctly attributed.

## 6. Vocabulary Classification

Vocabulary and expressions should be classified according to their learning purpose.

**ACTIVE**

Expressions learners should understand and use independently.

**UNDERSTAND**

Expressions learners should recognize and understand in conversations or reading, but do not necessarily need to use actively.

**PASSIVE**

Additional or specialized expressions that may be useful for recognition but are not current learning priorities.

Where appropriate, lessons should also identify lexical status:

- Neutral.
- Informal.
- Colloquial.
- Regional.
- Southern-associated.
- Formal.
- Literary.
- Slang.
- Dated or uncommon.

These labels should describe usage rather than imply that one variety is inherently superior to another.

## 7. Lesson Feedback and Correction Policy

Feedback should help learners improve without confusing grammatical correctness with stylistic preference.

The standard feedback sequence is:

### Correction

Identify genuine grammatical, lexical, or structural errors.

### Natural Reformulation

Offer a more natural or contextually appropriate version where useful.

### Recall

Ask the learner to reproduce or independently reuse the improved expression.

The feedback should distinguish:

1. A genuine grammatical error.
2. An incorrect or unsuitable word choice.
3. A grammatically correct but unnatural formulation.
4. A correct expression that can be made more conversational.
5. An alternative that changes tone, emphasis, or meaning.

Not every learner sentence requires correction.

Sometimes the most useful feedback is simply to confirm that the learner has expressed the idea correctly and naturally.

## 8. Cultural and Regional Accuracy

American English is not culturally or linguistically uniform.

The United States contains substantial regional, social, ethnic, generational, and situational variation.

The course therefore avoids treating one speaker, one region, or one social group as representative of the entire country.

Southern English is included as a meaningful regional dimension of American English.

Its expressions, pronunciation, rhythm, and conversational features should be presented with appropriate context.

The modern Southern flavor used in the course is intended to enrich communication, not to turn the learner into a caricature of a Southern speaker.

The course prioritizes:

- Contemporary relevance.
- Natural conversational use.
- Appropriate tone.
- Regional awareness.
- Respect for linguistic diversity.

## 9. Lesson Reference Notes

Each lesson should include a compact reference section when external sources have materially informed its linguistic or cultural explanations.

The reference notes should identify:

- The source consulted.
- The specific purpose for which it was used.
- The relevant topic or language feature, where practical.

For example:

**Reference Notes**

- Grammar: Cambridge English Grammar Today.
- American vocabulary: Merriam-Webster.
- Usage patterns: COCA, where applicable.
- Regional variation: relevant academic sources on Southern American English.

References must be genuine and relevant.

A source should not be listed merely because it appears in the project's general bibliography.

When a lesson contains original dialogues or invented situations, these should be identified as educational examples rather than attributed to reference sources.

---

## 10. Limitations and Scope

American English Method is a practical educational framework.

It is not intended to:

- Replace comprehensive academic linguistics research.
- Establish new grammatical rules.
- Claim that one American dialect is the only correct form of English.
- Guarantee that every expression is universally accepted.
- Present cultural observations as fixed characteristics of all Americans.
- Claim formal validation or accreditation without an independent assessment process.

Language changes over time.

Regional expressions evolve, conversational preferences shift, and new forms emerge.

For this reason, lessons and reference notes may be reviewed and updated when better evidence or more relevant usage information becomes available.

The project values transparency about uncertainty and limitations.

## 11. Quality Assurance Checklist

Before publication, each lesson should be reviewed against the following checklist.

### Language

- [ ] Are the examples grammatically sound?
- [ ] Are the expressions appropriate for the intended context?
- [ ] Are natural alternatives distinguished from actual corrections?
- [ ] Are important differences in meaning explained?
- [ ] Are regional, formal, informal, and dated expressions identified where necessary?

### Authenticity

- [ ] Does the dialogue represent a plausible contemporary situation?
- [ ] Does the language sound natural rather than mechanically translated?
- [ ] Are contractions and connected speech used appropriately?
- [ ] Is Southern influence included only where it adds value?

### Cultural Accuracy

- [ ] Are cultural claims appropriately qualified?
- [ ] Are stereotypes avoided?
- [ ] Are regional differences respected?
- [ ] Are interpretations distinguished from documented facts?

### Learning Value

- [ ] Does the learner have an opportunity to produce original language?
- [ ] Is vocabulary classified by learning priority?
- [ ] Is there a meaningful practice task?
- [ ] Can feedback follow the established learning cycle?

### References

- [ ] Have relevant reference sources been consulted?
- [ ] Are source descriptions accurate?
- [ ] Are references relevant to the actual claims?
- [ ] Are original educational examples clearly distinguished from sourced material?

## 12. Project Commitment

American English Method is committed to practical communication, linguistic accuracy, contextual understanding, and continuous improvement.

The project does not seek to impose artificial rules on a living language.

It seeks to help learners understand how English works, recognize how people actually use it, and express their own thoughts with greater confidence and precision.

**Our standard:**

> Correctness without rigidity. Naturalness without imitation. Regional flavor without stereotypes. Communication with meaning.

**American English Method**

*Learn the language. Understand the context. Express yourself naturally.*
